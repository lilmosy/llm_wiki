## C9  (10 entities, sources: m043)
**members:** 12 nautical miles, 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, Territorial waters, airspace, baseline, coastal state, innocent passage, maritime delimitation, seabed, transit passage

This community centers on the legal concept of territorial waters (also called the territorial sea), defined as the belt of coastal waters extending at most 12 nautical miles (equivalent to 22.2 km or 13.8 mi) from the baseline of a coastal state. This maximum extent and the definition of territorial waters are established by the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, an international treaty from 1982. The baseline used to measure territorial waters is usually the mean low-water mark of a coastal state, and this baseline belongs to that coastal state. Territorial waters are regarded as the sovereign territory of the coastal state from which they are measured, and this sovereignty extends beyond the waters themselves to include the airspace above the territorial sea and the seabed below it. Despite this sovereignty, territorial waters allow certain rights of passage for foreign vessels: innocent passage permits foreign civilian ships to pass through, while transit passage specifically applies to passage through straits within territorial waters. Additionally, territorial waters are subject to maritime delimitation, the term in international law for the process of adjusting territorial sea boundaries between states.
